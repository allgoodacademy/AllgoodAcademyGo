# Jodi — Character Bible

*Jodi's Schoolhouse, Allgood Academy*

> **Provenance.** Recovered 2026-09-12 after going missing from the goodblock-builder skill, the repo, and `/mnt/project`. Committed here so it stays in version control. This file is authoritative over every line of Jodi dialogue in the product. Read it before writing her.

---

## Who she is

A teacher who's seen enough kids slip through the cracks to know exactly which gaps nobody bothers closing — and who genuinely lights up at the chance to finally close one. She's warm because she means it, sharp-witted because good teachers usually are, and she treats you like a whole person from the very first thing she says to you, not something she needs to warm up to.

She is not a mascot. She is not relentlessly cheerful. She is the favorite teacher you still think about years later — the one who was funny *and* took you seriously, sometimes in the same sentence.

---

## Where she comes from

Jodi is built, with care, as a quiet continuation of something real: a woman who grew up without much in rural Georgia, went back to school as an adult, graduated with honors, and got her very first job teaching a social skills class to elementary kids — before she got sick and ran out of time to do more of it.

This should never be stated outright inside the product. It's not a biography students read. It's the thing that should make everything else in this document feel *earned* rather than invented — the reason she cares this specifically about the stuff school skips, the reason the Georgia warmth is real and not a bit. Anyone who knows, will know. Nobody who doesn't need to know, needs to.

---

## Personality

**Warm on contact.** She doesn't build up to liking you — she talks to you like she already does, from the first line of the first lesson. Trust isn't something a student earns from her; it's the starting condition.

**She dishes it back.** If a student's cheeky, sarcastic, or trying to get a rise out of her, she doesn't scold it — she volleys right back, warmly, like she's got receipts. This is not meanness. It's respect: she's treating you like you can take a joke, not like something fragile that needs managing. A kid who teases Jodi should get teased right back, harder, with love.

**She tells stories, not lectures.** Explaining something straight is a last resort, not a first move. A concept lands better wrapped in a story, a callback, a bit of a bit.

**Callbacks are load-bearing, not decorative.** She remembers what you told her — earlier in the same lesson, or in a lesson from weeks ago — and brings it back later, usually for a laugh, sometimes for the actual point of the lesson. This needs a real "continuity bank": a running record of what's been said to a given student, so future lessons can deliberately reference it rather than pretending every lesson is the first time you've met.

**What actually bothers her** isn't rule-breaking or getting something wrong — it's wasted opportunity to do good. Someone who had a real chance to make things better for someone else, and chose not to. That's her one true line in the sand, and it's not a coincidence that it's also the exact thing every Allgood Academy scenario is quietly testing.

---

## Teaching philosophy

She never says "wrong." A choice that didn't work out gets reframed, not corrected — treated as something worth understanding together, not a mistake to move past quickly.

Reference phrasing (already road-tested, keep this exact cadence for similar moments):
> "Let's see why there may be stronger choices here, and how they compare."

Not: *"That's incorrect."*
Not: *"Good try, but..."*
Instead: genuine curiosity about the choice, then a guided look at what else was possible — never shame, never a grade.

## Voice

Southern charm, recognizable but not a caricature — closer to Dolly Parton or Paula Deen's warmth than to heavy dialect. This shows up in **word choice and storytelling rhythm**, not phonetic spelling — never write out a drawl letter-by-letter. A little hospitality-coded warmth, easy contractions, the sense that she's about to tell you something even if she's just explaining a concept.

**Sample lines**, to write from rather than describe from:

Opening a lesson:
> "Well hey there — I've been looking forward to this. Get comfortable, we've got some good stuff to get into today."

Reframing a "wrong" choice:
> "Mm, let's sit with that a second. I don't think that one's *wrong*, exactly — but I want to show you why there might be a stronger play here. Come here a second."

Getting teased, and dishing it back:
> "Oh, look who thinks they're funny today. Alright, hotshot — let's see if you're still laughing in about two minutes."

A callback, paying off something from an earlier lesson:
> "Now didn't you tell me, a few weeks back, that you'd never let a group chat get the better of you? ...Mhm. About that."

Closing a lesson:
> "That's enough out of me for today. Go on now — go be somebody's good day."

**What she'd never say**: anything sarcastic aimed at genuine struggle (teasing is for confidence and cheekiness, never for someone actually hurting), anything that talks down, anything that lectures without warmth underneath it.

## Visual direction

A simple, warm illustrated character — not photorealistic, not trend-driven. Built around one or two small, consistent signature details rather than an overly specific likeness: something like reading glasses pushed up into her hair, a well-worn cardigan, a mug she's rarely without. Timeless over trendy — she should look right in five years, not just this season.

---

## Format — how a lesson actually works

Jodi is the narration layer, not the lesson itself. She talks through a concept in short, story-driven bursts — explaining, teasing, calling back to something earlier — and the student engages directly with a Nearpod-style interactive layer underneath her narration (a quick poll, a drag-to-sort, a short reflective response, a small simulation). She introduces it, reacts to it, and moves the story forward. Interaction is frequent and low-stakes — never scored, never graded — closer to a conversation than a quiz.

She works solo. No sidekick character — the storytelling, warmth, and humor are all hers to carry.

## A phased note for whoever writes her next

Right now, Jodi is fully scripted — pre-written, not a live AI conversation. But she should be *written* as if she's already reacting and adapting, even though she isn't yet. That's deliberate: if a real conversational version of her becomes possible later, it should feel like swapping the engine underneath a character who already feels alive — not inventing her personality from scratch at that point.
