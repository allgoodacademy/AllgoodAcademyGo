Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 27, 2026

This policy explains what Allgood Academy collects, why, and what we will never do with it. It covers everything on allgoodacademy.com: lessons, games, simulations, Spanish stories, the dashboard and the teacher tools. It applies to students, teachers and school staff alike.

Request a review or deletion

What changed on November 27, 2026. This policy now describes how students actually use Allgood Academy today: no account to start, an optional first name or nickname when they join a class or save, and two ways to save progress (a save code, or Google for students 13 and older). It replaces the Recruit Codes we no longer use, and it adds student messages and the Help Center. We also changed how long we keep student data (Section 8): once a year, we delete student records that haven't been used in the past 2 years.

The short version

The full policy is below. This is the honest summary of it.

  • We never sell your data, and we never use it for advertising. There is no advertising anywhere in Allgood Academy.
  • Students never need an account. Every lesson starts without signing in. We never ask a student for a last name, an email address or a birthday.
  • The only name a student gives us is a first name or nickname, typed when they join a class or save their progress. Saving is optional: a save code, or Google for students 13 and older.
  • Teachers sign in with Google or with an email and password, because they're adults setting up their own classes.
  • Students can type free text in two places: an optional comment at the end of a lesson, and a message to us. Section 3 explains exactly what happens to both.
  • You can ask us to review or delete a specific student's data at any time, and we'll do it.

1. Who this policy covers

Different people use Allgood Academy, and we collect very different things from each. Most of the confusion about student privacy comes from policies that blur these together, so we've kept them separate throughout.

  • Students. No account, at any age. A student can join a class with a class code and a first name or nickname, and can choose to save their progress.
  • Teachers and school staff. Sign in with Google or with an email and password to create and manage classes.
  • Visitors to our public pages, such as the homepage and For teachers.

When anyone opens a lesson, our sign-in service quietly creates an anonymous ID for that browser so progress can be kept. It carries no name, no email and nothing else that identifies a person.

2. What we collect

From students

  • An anonymous ID, created automatically as described above.
  • A first name or nickname, if they choose to give one, when they join a class or save their progress. We ask for no last names, and it can be up to 24 characters. A teacher sees this name on their class list.
  • If they save with a save code: the code (three words and four numbers) is made on their own device. It becomes a sign-in credential held by our sign-in service. It is never stored in our database, and teachers can't see it. A copy stays in that browser so the student can look it up again.
  • If they're 13 or older and save with Google: Google shares their name, email address and profile picture with our sign-in service so they can sign back in on another device. We use these only to sign them in, and to reply if they send us a message.
  • What they did: choices and answers inside lessons, games, simulations and Spanish stories; scores; completion; how far they got; time spent; which class they joined; topics they told us they want to work on; and whether a Help Center article helped them (never the words they searched for).

Before a student saves, we ask whether they're 13 or older. That's only to decide which way of saving to offer.

From teachers and school staff

Name and email address (from Google, or as typed when creating an email-and-password account), the classes you create, the students who joined them, your plan (free or Pro), and your email preferences. When a teacher searches the Help Center from the dashboard we keep the search words, trimmed to 80 characters with anything that looks like an email address or a long number removed, so we can write the help people are missing. Before creating a class for the first time, a teacher is shown a plain-language notice and must confirm they are authorized staff using this tool under their school's policies.

3. Free text, and the two places it exists

There are two places where a student types words that we store:

  • An optional comment (and star rating) at the end of a lesson.
  • A message to us, sent from the dashboard's Comms button or the Español hub's idea box. Messages from the Español hub are also emailed to our inbox so a person reads them quickly.

We can't inspect what someone chooses to write, so we are explicit about how we handle it:

  • Both are stored with the student's anonymous ID, so that a request to delete a specific child's words can actually be honored.
  • They can be read only by a small number of named Allgood Academy administrators. They are never shown to other students, and they are not shown to teachers.
  • They are never used for advertising, never sold, and never shared outside Allgood Academy.

We ask students not to include their full name or any personal detail. If you believe a child has entered something identifying, email us and we will delete it (see Section 9).

4. What is stored on the device

To keep things working between page loads and visits, we keep a small amount of data in the browser's Local Storage on the device itself. This includes the session's sign-in state, the student's chosen name, which lessons they've completed, a save code if they made one (so the dashboard can show it again), and which Help Center articles were read recently.

This data stays on the device. Clearing your browser's site data removes it. On a shared device, such as a school Chromebook, signing out or clearing site data is what stops the next person from picking up where the last one left off.

5. How we use it

  • To run the learning experience. Showing a student their own progress and badges, and restoring it on a return visit or another device.
  • To give teachers visibility into their own classes. A teacher can see progress for the students in a class they own, and only that class.
  • To improve the lessons and the help. We look at aggregated patterns, for example that most students missed a particular question or that an article didn't help, to fix what isn't working.
  • To keep accounts working and secure. Maintaining sessions and signing people in.
  • To send teachers occasional product updates. If you're a teacher with an account, we may email you now and then about new simulations, lessons and features. Every email tells you how to stop, and you can turn updates off in your dashboard anytime. We never send these emails to students.
  • To send teachers service emails about their own classes. When you create a class we may email you your class code and how to get started. On Fridays we may send a short report on your class's week, only if your students did something that week. You can turn the weekly report off with one click in any report or in your dashboard. These aren't marketing emails and don't depend on the product-updates choice.

6. What we never do

  • We do not sell personal data to anyone, under any circumstances.
  • We do not use student data for advertising, and we do not show advertising in Allgood Academy.
  • We do not build behavioral or marketing profiles of students.
  • We do not require any student to give a last name, an email address, a birthday or any other real-world identifying detail in order to learn.
  • We do not send marketing or product email to students.
  • We do not put student data into AI tools. Some lessons use AI-made character voices, recorded ahead of time from our own scripts (see Section 10).

Allgood Academy is free for students, permanently. That is a product commitment, and it is part of why none of the above is ever on the table.

7. Age (COPPA)

No account is needed at any age. Saving with Google is offered only to students who tell us they're 13 or older. Younger students save with a save code instead, which needs no email address and no account with anyone else.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 beyond a first name or nickname, which is optional, and the learning activity described in Section 2. If we learn that a student under 13 saved with a Google account, we will remove that Google link and the information that came with it.

A save code works like a password. Anyone who has the exact code can open that student's progress. That's deliberately how a student moves to another device without an email address. Keep it private, don't share it in class, and tell us if one needs to be retired.

8. How long we keep student data

We keep student data to a minimum. A student is identified by an anonymous ID, plus the first name or nickname they chose if they joined a class or saved. We never ask for a last name, an email address or a birthday. The only exception is the Google account a student 13 or older may choose to save with.

We keep a student's progress for as long as it's in use, so a student who comes back next school year still has their work. That's the reason we keep it: so students and their teachers can pick up where they left off.

Once a year, we delete student records that haven't been used in the past 2 years. That includes their name or nickname, progress, choices and answers, session and event logs, comments, messages, class membership and their sign-in (save code or Google). We'll also delete a specific student's data anytime a parent, teacher or school asks (see Section 9).

9. Requesting a review or deletion

If you are a parent, guardian, teacher or school administrator and you want to review or delete a specific student's data, email us and we will take care of it. You do not need an account to make this request, and you do not need to explain why.

Tell us what you can: the student's first name or nickname, the class code, the teacher's name or the approximate dates. We will find the records and confirm in writing once they are removed. Please don't email us a save code. We don't need it, and it works like a password.

10. Third-party services

We keep this list short on purpose, and we name everything on it.

  • Google Firebase: sign-in, database and hosting. The student and teacher records described in this policy are stored here. Its sign-in service holds sign-in details such as a teacher's email address, a save-code sign-in, or the Google account a student 13 or older saved with.
  • Google sign-in: used only when a teacher, or a student 13 or older, chooses "Continue with Google."
  • Stripe: teacher Pro payments are processed by Stripe, and Allgood Academy never sees or stores card numbers. Students never pay.
  • Google Analytics: used on our public pages (the homepage, For teachers, About and How I build) to understand how people find us, on the teacher sign-in page, and on the signed-in dashboard only for teacher accounts. It never runs on any page a student uses, and it never loads unconditionally. Separately, our own lesson records note where a visit came from (a link tag such as "email", or the name of the referring website). This uses no cookies and nothing that identifies a person.
  • ElevenLabs (AI voices): some lessons and stories use character voices made with ElevenLabs. We send ElevenLabs only our own scripts. The audio is recorded ahead of time and played from our own servers, so no student data, and no request from a student's device, ever reaches ElevenLabs.

Our lessons, games and stories load their code, fonts and audio from our own servers rather than from third-party content networks, so a student working through a lesson is not making requests to other companies.

11. Schools, districts and teachers

When a teacher creates a class and shares its class code, students who join it appear on that teacher's class list under the first name or nickname they typed. A teacher sees only the classes they own. Teachers see the steps and choices of students in their own class. In the Español stories, that includes which option a student picked first at each step, never anything they typed.

We do not ask a school to provide consent on parents' behalf, and we do not rely on a school having done so. We designed Allgood Academy so that a student never has to give us identifying information to learn. A teacher or staff member using Allgood Academy with students remains responsible for doing so in line with their school's and district's own policies.

Schools and districts that need a written agreement, a data-processing addendum, or answers for a vendor review should email us. We would rather answer a procurement questionnaire than have you guess from this page.

12. Data security

Access to learner records is restricted at the database level: a teacher can read only their own classroom's roster, administrative access is limited to a named allowlist, and a learner can read only their own progress. We implement industry-standard security measures to protect your data. However, no method of transmission over the Internet is 100% secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

13. Changes to this policy

When we change this policy we update the "Last updated" date at the top. That date reflects the last time the words on this page actually changed — it is not generated automatically, so you can use it to tell whether anything is different since you last read it.

If we make a change that materially affects what we collect from learners or how long we keep it, we will say so plainly at the top of this page rather than relying on you to notice.

14. Contact us

Questions about this policy, a deletion request, or a district vendor review all go to the same place: learning@allgoodacademy.com. A real person reads it.
