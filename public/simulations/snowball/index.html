The Snowball: a savings simulation

The Snowball
Example growth rate · not financial advice
Middle: money put inSnow around it: growth

For teachers

Grades 7–9 · About 10–15 minutes · Projector (Present mode) or Chromebooks. No sign-in, and nothing students type is saved.

Standards and sources
  • National Standards for Personal Financial Education (CEE & Jump$tart, 2021): Saving 8-4 (principal vs. interest, Rule of 72), Saving 8-5 (compound vs. simple interest), Investing 8-6 (risk differs by investment), Investing 8-7 (compounding rewards investing regularly over longer periods).
  • CCSS Math: 7.RP.A.3 (percent and simple interest), 8.F.A.3 (linear vs. nonlinear functions). Grade 9 stretch: HSF-LE.A.1 and HSF-LE.A.3 (linear vs. exponential growth).
  • CASEL: Responsible Decision-Making (anticipating and evaluating the consequences of choices).
  • Background reading: Investor.gov: What is compound interest? (U.S. SEC) and CFPB youth financial education.

How to read the snowball: its size shows the money in it. The dark middle is money put in; the white snow around it is growth. Every number uses a steady example growth rate (6%) so the pattern is easy to see. People, cards, and amounts are made up. This is a math lesson, not investment advice.
