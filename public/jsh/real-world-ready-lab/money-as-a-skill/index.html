The Impulse Window

You've been saving for headphones — $60. You've got $58. You're almost there. Then you spot something else: a phone case for $12. You can afford it right now. It sets your savings back to $46.

1What do you do? Buy it, wait, or ask to see the actual maths first. Thinking it through isn't a decision yet — you still have to make the call after.

The impulse window

Most impulse buys feel most urgent in the first twenty minutes. Two days later, most people want the thing much less — or not at all. And this one doesn't just cost $12. It puts you $14 back from a goal you were $2 from. That's probably two more weeks.

Now — with that on the table. What do you do?

"You're not choosing between buying it or not. You're choosing how much your future self gets a vote."

The Ask

You're at a local shop. You find a phone case you actually want — it's $20. When you pick it up you notice the packaging is dented and one corner has a small scuff. Not broken. Just not pristine. The tag still says $20.

1What do you do? Pay it, ask, or look at the worst case first. Looking isn't a decision — you still have to make one after.

The ask maths

Best case: they say yes, or meet you in the middle.
Realistic: they say no. Nothing changes.
Worst case: a slightly awkward fifteen seconds. Then it's over.

Now what?

"The answer is always no until you ask. And 'no' is exactly where you already were."
