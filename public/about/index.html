Someone should have taught you this.

That sentence is the whole company. Most of what determines how a young person's life actually goes — how they handle money, conflict, a group chat, their own reputation — gets learned by accident, or painfully, or not at all. We think it can be taught on purpose, and that it should be free.

We're building the thing we wish someone had handed us at twelve.

Nobody sat me down and explained how a group chat actually works, what a purchase really costs, or why being right in front of an audience doesn't get you anywhere. I learned all of it the slow way.

Those aren't soft skills. They're the ones that decide how a life goes. They can be taught deliberately, and there's no good reason a kid should have to pay to learn them.

— William Allgood, founder
Message me on LinkedIn

Allgood Academy makes short, interactive lessons on life skills. Digital decisions came first — group chats, privacy, reputation — because that's where the stakes are highest right now and the guidance is thinnest. It isn't where we stop.

The model is simple and not negotiable: free for learners, forever. Revenue, when it comes, comes from tools that help educators and administrators do their jobs. Never from charging someone for access to their own learning. A student who can't pay is exactly the student this was built for.

The standard we hold ourselves to is craft. A lesson should feel like something made with care by people who took the subject seriously — interactive, well-built, and respectful of whoever's on the other side of it. Not a worksheet with a login.

OUR ONE NON-NEGOTIABLE

Jodi never grades. She reframes.

Jodi is the teacher inside every lesson. When you make a choice she doesn't put a red X on it — she explains the mechanism you couldn't see from where you were standing. The feeling we're after isn't mastery, and it isn't fun for its own sake. It's someone finally taught me this. Relief, not a score.

"Here's a trap I see people walk into all the time, and it's a sneaky one, because it sounds like good reasoning."

Jodi, in Money as a Skill — Case 4

Trust is the product

Everything else is downstream of a parent, a teacher, and a student all believing we're straight with them. When mission and money genuinely conflict, mission wins. That isn't idealism — it's the business model.

We don't claim what we can't show

If the data doesn't exist yet to back a claim, we don't make the claim. No invented statistics, no borrowed credibility, no badge we haven't earned. You'll hear about our gaps from us first.

Student safety isn't a feature

COPPA, FERPA and SOPPA are the floor we build on, not a compliance box. No ads, no data sold, no email collected from anyone under 13. There's no growth number that changes this.
